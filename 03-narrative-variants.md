# Narrative variants

Different versions of Rui's story for different contexts. All true, all consistent — just different lengths and angles.

## 30-second (recruiter call, networking intro)

> I'm a senior creative operations leader who spent 15 years at FARFETCH scaling its creative engine from Production Manager to SVP. I ran a 900-person global organization, a €50M+ P&L, and the production of 3.6M digital assets a year. My work cut operational spend from 10% to 1% of sales using Lean, AI automation, and Virtual Model technology — without dropping luxury quality. I trained originally as a textile and graphic designer, which is why I understand craft and operations both. I keep a small side venture — a collectible furniture studio — as a labor of love when time allows; it keeps my creative mind sharp. Now I'm looking for my next senior creative operations role.

## 15-second (LinkedIn about, cold outreach opener)

> Senior Creative Operations leader, ex-SVP at FARFETCH. Scaled a 900-person global creative org, managed a €50M+ P&L, cut op spend from 10%→1% of sales with Lean + AI. Keep a small furniture studio as a side venture. Now looking for my next senior ops role.

## Cover letter / outreach paragraph (the gap, framed)

> After 15 years building FARFETCH's creative operations, I needed space to breathe and rebuild — and I'd carried a creative idea for a long time. So I founded Rui Silva Studio, a collectible furniture practice where I imagine, design, and build pieces until they're done. It's a labor of love I keep running when time allows — it keeps my creative mind sharp and informs how I think about creative operations. What it confirmed for me is that my real edge is at scale — I love the craft, but I create the most value leading the creative engine that makes craft possible at industrial scale. That's what I'm looking to do next.

## Interview answer: "Why did you leave Farfetch / what have you been doing?"

> After 15 years at FARFETCH I needed space to breathe and rebuild. I'd also carried a creative idea for a long time — furniture as collectible craft — and I wanted to test it with my own hands, not just manage others making things. So I built a workshop, designed a credenza for my own living room, and the feedback made me keep going. It became Rui Silva Studio.
>
> The studio is a side venture, a labor of love I keep running when time allows. It's where I imagine, design, and build until something is done, and where I get to love all the challenges in between. It keeps my creative mind sharp. What it also did was confirm something I already suspected: my real edge is at scale. I love the craft, but the place I create the most value is leading the creative engine that makes craft possible at industrial scale. That's what I'm looking to do next.

## Interview answer: "Tell me about yourself"

> I started in textile and graphic design, then joined FARFETCH early and spent 15 years there, rising from Production Manager to SVP of Creative Operations. For most of those years I built the engine in the analog, non-AI world — scaling production, teams, and processes the hard way. Then in the last 4-5 years we started the AI and automation journey, and that changed everything — Virtual Model tech, ML translation, 80% automation, cutting spend from 10% to 1% of sales. That experience was incredible and it's what I need to continue.
>
> The last 3 years became almost entirely about cost-cutting — the company was fighting for survival. My creative brain was drained. I'm a builder, not a cost-cutter. So I took time off to breathe and feed my brain again — through a furniture studio where I could imagine, design, and build with my own hands. That labor of love restored what the final years had depleted, and it confirmed what I already knew: I love the craft, but my real edge is at scale, especially now with AI transforming what's possible. That's what I'm looking to do next.
