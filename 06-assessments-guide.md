# Leadership assessments — extraction guide

Rui will upload photos of three paper assessments. This file documents what each one is, what it measures, and exactly what to extract from the photo. The extracted data goes into `05-strengths.md`.

---

## 1. Leadership Circle Profile (LCP)

**Source:** leadershipcircle.com
**What it is:** A 360° leadership assessment that maps reactive vs. creative leadership styles. Ranks 18 dimensions in two domains:
- **Creative Competencies** (top half of circle): Relating, Self-Aware, Authenticity, Systems Awareness, Achieving, Purposeful Vision, Strategic Focus, Engagement Development, Collaborative, Mentoring, Balancing Results & Process, Team Leadership, Decisiveness, Visionary, Integrity, Interpersonal, Body/Mind/Spirit, Focus.
- **Reactive Tendencies** (bottom half): Complying, Protecting, Controlling — each with sub-traits: Autocratic, Ambition-addicted, Arrogant, Critical, Perfect, Distance, Striving, Self-critical, Victim, Pleasing, Belonging, Conservative, Cautious.

**Output you'll see on paper:**
- A circular diagram with 18 wedge scores (percentile ranks).
- Two aggregate scores: **Creative** (high = good) and **Reactive** (low = good).
- A **Leadership Culture Index** — Creative vs Reactive balance.
- Sometimes narrative pages: strengths, development areas, reactive triggers.

**What to extract from the photo:**
1. The top 5 **highest Creative dimensions** (percentile + name).
2. The top 3 **highest Reactive dimensions** (these are the development edges — overused strengths).
3. The **Creative aggregate** and **Reactive aggregate** scores.
4. Any self-vs-others gap (360° shows how Rui sees himself vs how others see him — big gaps = blind spots).
5. Any narrative text on the page (development themes, recurring feedback).

**Why it matters for Rui's search:**
- Creative Competencies → language for "leadership strengths" in CV/outreach.
- Reactive Tendencies → honest development areas for interviews ("What's a blind spot?").
- The 360° gap → self-awareness story (esp. if Rui rated himself differently than others did).

## 2. DiSC Classic 2.0

**Source:** internalchange.com / Wiley
**What it is:** A behavioural style assessment that places you on two axes (Assertiveness vs Receptiveness; Fast-paced vs Moderate-paced) and returns one of 4 (or blended) styles:
- **D (Dominance):** Direct, results-focused, decisive, strong-willed. Fast-paced + assertive.
- **i (influence):** Outgoing, enthusiastic, optimistic, people-oriented. Fast-paced + receptive.
- **S (Steadiness):** Even-tempered, accommodating, patient, humble. Moderate-paced + receptive.
- **C (Conscientiousness):** Analytical, reserved, precise, private. Moderate-paced + assertive.

**Output you'll see on paper:**
- A **classical profile graph** — 4 bars showing your score on each of D, i, S, C (adapted vs natural style, sometimes 3 lines).
- A **style blend** label (e.g., "High C / Moderate D" or a 2-3 letter pattern).
- Narrative pages describing:
  - Your tendencies / motivations / preferred environment.
  - What you bring to the organization.
  - What may hinder you (development areas).
  - How you react under stress / how others see you.

**What to extract from the photo:**
1. The **scores on all 4 scales** (D, i, S, C) — natural and adapted if shown.
2. The **primary + secondary style** (the blend).
3. Narrative: "What you bring to the organization" → leadership strengths language.
4. Narrative: "What may hinder you" → honest development areas.
5. Stress / conflict response pattern (esp. how Rui shows up under pressure).

**Why it matters for Rui's search:**
- Primary style predicts team-fit + hiring-manager-fit. A high-D or high-C creative-ops leader is very different in interview than a high-S.
- "What may hinder you" → pre-empt the predictable interview question.
- Style blend → tailor communication in outreach (e.g., high-C → lead with data/numbers, high-i → lead with energy/people).

## 3. CliftonStrengths (formerly StrengthsFinder)

**Source:** gallup.com/cliftonstrengths
**What it is:** Gallup's talent assessment that returns your top 5 (or full 34) themes from 34 possibilities, grouped in 4 domains:
- **Executing:** Achiever, Arranger, Belief, Consistency, Deliberative, Discipline, Focus, Responsibility, Restorative.
- **Influencing:** Activator, Command, Communication, Competition, Maximizer, Self-Assurance, Significance, Woo.
- **Relationship Building:** Adaptability, Connectedness, Developer, Empathy, Harmony, Includer, Individualization, Positivity, Relator.
- **Strategic Thinking:** Analytical, Context, Futuristic, Ideation, Intellection, Learner, Input, Strategic.

**Output you'll see on paper:**
- A **Top 5 Themes** list (most common report) OR a full 34 rank.
- Each theme with a short personalized description (Gallup's custom paragraph for that individual).
- Sometimes a domain dominance summary (which of the 4 domains you lead with).

**What to extract from the photo:**
1. The **Top 5 themes in order** (most important — this is the headline).
2. The **domain distribution** (does Rui lead with Executing / Influencing / Relationship / Strategic?).
3. Each theme's short description (Gallup's custom paragraph — these are gold for CV language).
4. Any theme insights (shared theme descriptions, action items).

**Why it matters for Rui's search:**
- Top 5 themes → the most authentic language for "what are your greatest strengths?" answers.
- Domain dominance → the leadership archetype (e.g., Executing + Strategic Thinking = operational strategist — matches Rui's career).
- Themes explain *why* Rui's achievements happened (Achiever explains the 10%→1% drive; Strategic explains the Virtual Model bet; Arranger/Discipline explains 4–5 day SLAs).

## Extraction plan when photos arrive

1. For each photo: identify the assessment, transcribe scores/themes exactly.
2. Cross-reference: where do the 3 assessments agree? (convergent strengths — high confidence).
3. Where do they complement? (one shows the what, another the why).
4. Where do they flag development edges? (DiSC "may hinder", LCP Reactive, CliftonStrengths = none by design — development = overused/balanced themes).
5. Write a unified "leadership style" section in `05-strengths.md`:
   - Confirmed strengths (with assessment evidence cited).
   - Leadership archetype (1-line summary across all 3).
   - Development areas (honest, prepped for interview).
   - CV/outreach language (the actual words to use).
