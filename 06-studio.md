## What the studio actually is

**Rui Silva Studio** — contemporary collectible furniture, founded May 2024, based in Guimarães (Rua Rio Ave, Prazins, 4800-615).

- **One-of-a-kind, handcrafted pieces** — not mass production. Made to order.
- **Lusitano Collection** — the debut collection, inspired by Portugal's Lusitano horse (strength, agility, practical beauty). Materials: oak, steel, brass, porcelain.
- **Pieces in the collection:** Credenza (media console), Bookshelf (sofa-side console), Coffee Table, Floor Lamp, Sofa Armrest.
- **Studio Partnership programme** — connects the studio with interior designers, architects, and creative professionals. Offers direct dialogue with Rui, creative previews, legacy certification (signed provenance digital ID), cultural exchange sessions.
- **Philosophy:** "Furniture as Cultural Infrastructure" — objects that last, tell stories, connect people. Manifesto: "We make fewer. We make better. We make personal."
- **Operational values:** sustainability, responsible sourcing, no unnecessary raw material stock, 6–16 week lead times, bespoke commissions welcome.

## The origin story (in Rui's words, from the site)

> "In early 2024, after 15 years in luxury fashion, I moved away to make space to breathe, rest and rebuild. At the time, I had just moved into a new house. Out back, I'd built a wooden workshop, mainly for woodworking as a hobby. One afternoon, I designed a credenza media console for my own living room. I wasn't expecting much — but the result was solid, and the feedback I got made me pause. That single piece reignited my creativity... I was reconnecting with the kind of work I thought I'd lost. Work that's hands-on. Built to last. Meant to be used. Meant to be kept."

## What it proves about Rui (for the job search)

This is the part that matters for the CV and interviews. The studio isn't a career detour — it's **evidence of a creative mindset that an operations leader brings to the table:**

1. **Creative imagination** — designs furniture collections from concept to finished piece. Futuristic #7 + Strategic #8 in action.
2. **End-to-end execution** — "imagine, design, implement until done." Manages the full product lifecycle: concept, design, materials sourcing, manufacturing, finishing, photography, website, brand, partnerships, fulfilment. This is the same end-to-end ownership as Creative Operations at FARFETCH — at boutique scale.
3. **Loves the challenges in between** — the Learner #3 theme. Every piece is a problem to solve: materials, joinery, finishes, supply chain, sustainability.
4. **Brand and narrative thinking** — built a brand with a manifesto ("Furniture as Cultural Infrastructure"), a collection story (Lusitano), a partnership programme, provenance certification. This is strategic brand thinking, not just making objects.
5. **Craft sensibility that informs operational decisions** — because Rui has his hands in the making, he understands creative work in a way pure-ops leaders don't. This is the "bridges artisanal quality with industrial-scale profitability" line from his CV, made tangible.
6. **Self-directed initiative** — built a workshop, taught himself woodworking, launched a brand, built a website, set up a partnership programme. No one told him to do any of this.
7. **Runs in parallel** — Rui will not close the studio. It runs alongside his operations career. It is the creative outlet that keeps his creative mind sharp.

### In interviews (the honest, framing-aware answer)

**Q: "Why did you leave Farfetch / what have you been doing?"**
> After 15 years at FARFETCH I needed space to breathe and rebuild. I'd also carried a creative idea for a long time — furniture as collectible craft — and I wanted to test it with my own hands, not just manage others making things. So I built a workshop, designed a credenza for my own living room, and the feedback made me keep going. It became Rui Silva Studio.
>
> The studio is a side venture, a labor of love I keep running when time allows. It's where I imagine, design, and build until something is done, and where I get to love all the challenges in between. It keeps my creative mind sharp. What it also did was confirm something I already suspected: my real edge is at scale. I love the craft, but the place I create the most value is leading the creative engine that makes craft possible at industrial scale. That's what I'm looking to do next.

### What NOT to say (the traps)

- ❌ Don't call it a "sabbatical" — it's an ongoing side venture, not a break.
- ❌ Don't call it a "failed startup" — it's a deliberate creative practice that continues.
- ❌ Don't say it "runs in parallel" — that can read as competing with the job. Say "side venture, when time allows."
- ❌ Don't apologise for it or minimise it — it's evidence of creative imagination + end-to-end execution, which most ops leaders don't have.
- ❌ Don't let it dominate the narrative — it's the *reinforcement* of a creative mindset, not the main story. The main story is 15 years scaling FARFETCH's creative engine.
