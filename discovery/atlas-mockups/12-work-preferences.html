Career direction / reflective preferences

Make your work preferences
visible to yourself.

There are no right answers and no personality label at the end. Choose what sounds more or less like the work you want next. Atlas will keep these choices editable and show where they influence a role comparison.

This is a reflection tool, not a psychological assessment. It does not measure ability, employability, or predict your future.

Three useful tensions

Choose an initial leaning—or leave any prompt unresolved. A useful uncertainty is better than a forced answer.

Where do you want your energy to go?

There can be more than one answer. Choose the activities you would like more of, not what you think sounds impressive.

Which conditions help you do good work?

Use this to make trade-offs explicit. It is not a claim that one environment is universally better.

What must a next move respect?

These are constraints, not weaknesses. They stop a theoretically attractive direction becoming an impractical recommendation.

Nothing here is permanent. You can revise this as you learn.

How Atlas uses this

As your stated input

Atlas records the selections you make. It does not infer a hidden personality type from them.

Alongside real evidence

Preferences do not override experience, qualifications, financial needs, location, or market evidence.

In the open

On every role comparison, you can see which of your choices were considered and switch them off.
