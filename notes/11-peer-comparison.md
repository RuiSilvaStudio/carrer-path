# Senior Creative Operations Leaders — LinkedIn Profile Comparison

**Purpose:** Compare how real senior creative-ops leaders present themselves on LinkedIn against Rui Silva's draft (ex-SVP Creative Operations, FARFETCH).

**Method:** LinkedIn profiles are behind an authwall and cannot be scraped directly. Data was gathered from Google search snippets (which surface headline + About excerpts), LinkedIn public directory listings, RocketReach career-history pages, and LinkedIn post/activity feeds that quote profile headlines. About-section quotes are captured where search engines indexed them; career histories come from RocketReach aggregations. This means headline + career data is reliable; About-section tone analysis is based on the subset where About text was indexed.

## 5 Concrete Adjustments for Rui's Draft

### 1. Move all hard numbers OUT of the About section; replace with a narrative opening.
**Why:** Zero peers lead their About with quantified metrics. Every indexed About opens with a first-person identity/positioning statement. Rui's current About (900 team, €50M P&L, 10%→1%, 3.6M assets) is the loudest in the peer set — it reads exactly like "trying too hard," the concern Rui already flagged for the headline.
**How:** Open with 1–2 sentences like: *"I lead creative operations at scale — building the production systems, teams, and workflows that let creative work ship globally without losing quality."* Then a brief narrative paragraph on approach. Move all metrics to Experience bullets (where they already are).

### 2. Add a short positioning tagline to the headline.
**Why:** ~40% of peers add a brief phrase after title/company. Jeff Steep: "Building the future of brand storytelling." This is the senior-peer convention for adding personality without numbers.
**How:** Something like: `Senior Creative Operations Leader | Ex-SVP, FARFETCH | Scaling creative at global scale` or `| Building creative systems that scale` — a phrase, not a metric.

### 3. Keep the headline metric-free — Rui's instinct was correct.
**Why:** No peer in the set puts numbers (headcount, P&L, %) in their headline. The decision to remove metrics from the headline is validated by all 12 profiles.
**Action:** No change needed; confirm this is the right call.

### 4. Consider naming 1–2 additional prior companies in the headline (if notable).
**Why:** Heather Hanrahan ("Ex Rivian & Atlassian | Ex Wieden+Kennedy") and Jeff Steep ("Nike, Aetna, Airbnb") both name multiple former employers in the headline. This signals breadth and cross-industry range — directly relevant for Rui, who is positioning around a transition.
**How:** If Rui has a notable company before FARFETCH (or a well-known brand he worked with), add it: `Senior Creative Operations Leader | Ex-SVP FARFETCH | Ex-[Company]`. Only do this if the prior company adds prestige signal; don't force it.

### 5. If Rui has side projects, ventures, or a gap period, list them openly — peers normalize this.
**Why:** Alissa Sheely's Ghana agency, Bianca's coaching certification (in her headline), and Heather Hanrahan's freelance period and yoga certification are all listed without apology or special framing. Senior peers treat these as part of their professional identity, not as things to hide.
**How:** Add side ventures as a separate Experience entry or in the About narrative. Frame matter-of-factly: "Founder, [X]" or "Independent advisor/consultant (2024–present)."

## Methodology Notes & Limitations
- LinkedIn profiles are behind an authwall; direct scraping failed (web_extract and browser_navigate both hit the login wall).
- Headlines were captured reliably from Google search snippets and LinkedIn directory pages.
- Career histories were captured from RocketReach aggregator pages (which mirror LinkedIn Experience sections).
- About-section text was only indexed by search engines for 3–4 profiles (Jeff Steep, Bianca Medina Cundari, Emily Goldmann). For the rest, About tone is inferred from headline style and public posts. The headline-level patterns (no numbers, "Ex" convention, taglines) are reliable across all 12.
- Michelle Rowe (FARFETCH → Fashion Nova) is the single closest comp to Rui but had the least indexed data. Her headline and career history are confirmed; About text was not accessible.
