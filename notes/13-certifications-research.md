## TIER 3: "Table Stakes" vs "Differentiators"

### Table Stakes (Expected at Director+ Level — NOT Differentiators)
- **MBA or equivalent executive education** — so common at VP/SVP level that not having one is more notable than having one
- **PMP** — widely held, expected in operations roles
- **CSM (Certified ScrumMaster)** — common, expected in Agile environments
- **LinkedIn Learning certificates** — everyone has access, carries minimal signal

### Differentiators (Rare and Valued)
- **MIT Professional Education — Applied Generative AI in Business Transformation** — appears 3 times among senior profiles; signals AI literacy at executive level
- **Google Cloud Certified Generative AI Leader** — emerging 2025–2026 credential; signals current AI knowledge
- **Adobe Workfront Expert/Certified Professional** (beyond Core) — specialized, valued in Content Supply Chain roles, signals hands-on platform knowledge
- **INSEAD / MIT Sloan / Stanford Executive Education certificates** — premium brand signal at SVP level
- **Lean Six Sigma Black Belt** (not Yellow/White) — signals serious process improvement commitment at scale
- **Coaching Certification** — niche but signals people leadership depth

## Methodology Notes & Limitations
- LinkedIn profiles are behind an authwall; direct scraping via web_extract failed (returns "Website Not Supported" error). All data comes from Google search snippets of LinkedIn profile pages and LinkedIn directory listings.
- "Licenses & Certifications" sections are only partially indexed by Google. Many profiles have certifications that simply weren't captured in search snippets. This means the frequency counts are likely **undercounts** — the actual frequency of PMP, Adobe Workfront, and AI certs may be higher than reported.
- The absence of a visible certification in a snippet does NOT mean the person doesn't have it. However, the pattern of which certs DO appear in snippets is reliable — these are the ones prominently displayed.
- Education data (universities, MBA programs) is more reliably indexed than certifications, so the MBA/executive education frequency is the most robust finding.
- The original 12 peers from the comparison file had very sparse certification data — this is itself a finding (senior leaders don't prominently display online course certs), but may also reflect indexing limitations.
- ~40+ profiles were examined across multiple search rounds; this is a substantial sample but not exhaustive.
