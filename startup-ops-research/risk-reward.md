# Risk-Reward Tradeoff: Startup vs Corporate Role for a Senior Ops Leader

## What Rui GAINS joining a startup

### 1. Equity upside (the primary reason)
- At Series B with 0.5-1.5% equity, a €200M exit yields €1-3M (post-dilution). A €1B exit yields €2.5-7.5M.
- This is wealth-building money that a corporate salary (even SVP) cannot match over 4-6 years.
- Corporate SVP comp: €200-300k base + 15-30% bonus = €230-390k all-in, no equity. Over 5 years: €1.15-1.95M, all cash, taxed as income.
- Startup scenario: €160k base + equity. Over 5 years: €800k cash + €1-5M equity if exit. Higher variance, higher ceiling.

### 2. Scope and ownership
- At a startup, Rui would own the entire creative/content operations function — build the org, choose the tools, set the strategy, hire the team.
- At a corporate role, he'd inherit an existing org and political structure. His FARFETCH experience of navigating 900-person, 5-country complexity is valuable but also constraining.
- Startups let him "build from zero" — which his furniture studio venture shows he enjoys.

### 3. Speed and impact
- Startups ship in weeks, not quarters. Rui's Kaizen/Lean process engineering can show ROI in months, not years.
- No 6-month approval cycles. No corporate governance drag. His "80% automation" playbook can be deployed fast.

### 4. AI-native operating model
- AI-content startups are building the tools and workflows that replace traditional creative production. Rui would be operating at the frontier of his own field (Virtual Model, ML translation).
- Corporate creative teams are often slower to adopt AI due to risk aversion and legacy vendor contracts. Startups give him a clean slate.

### 5. Network and signaling
- Working at a VC-backed startup puts Rui in the VC/founder network — valuable for future roles, advisory positions, or his own venture.
- Ex-FARFETCH + successful startup exit = strong founder/COO credentials for his next chapter.

### 6. Learning and adaptability
- 16 months unemployed means his corporate currency is depreciating. A startup role restarts the clock, rebuilds recent relevance, and proves he can operate outside a big-brand cocoon.

## What Rui LOSES joining a startup

### 1. Cash haircut (real and immediate)
- SVP-level corporate comp in Europe: €200-300k base + bonus + benefits.
- Startup Series B Head of Ops: €150-200k base, minimal/no bonus, thinner benefits.
- Net annual cash loss: €30-100k/year. Over 4 years: €120-400k in foregone cash.
- This is the "price" of the equity lottery ticket.

### 2. Job security
- ~50% of Series B startups don't reach Series C. If the startup fails to raise or shuts down, Rui is job-hunting again — with a shorter runway and a startup failure on his CV (which can be spun as learning, but is still a gap).
- Corporate roles have layoffs too, but the base rate of startup failure is higher (Carta: 0.6-1.3 hires per departure across sectors; 50%+ of funded startups eventually stall).

### 3. Structure and resources
- No 900-person team. No established vendor relationships. No mature finance/Legal/HR functions to lean on.
- Rui would build everything from scratch — which is energizing but also means he's doing IC work alongside leadership, especially at Series A/B.

### 4. Benefits and stability
- Portugal has strong statutory benefits, but startup private health/pension/leave are often thinner than corporate equivalents.
- No car allowance, no executive perks, no large L&D budget.

### 5. Political capital / brand association
- FARFETCH is a globally recognized brand. A Series B startup is unknown. "Ex-SVP FARFETCH" carries more weight on a CV than "Ex-Head of Ops at [startup no one's heard of]."
- If the startup fails, the brand association doesn't transfer. If it succeeds, it does — but that's a 4-7 year bet.

### 6. Work intensity
- Startups expect senior leaders to work harder and more unpredictably than corporates. Rui has done 15 years at FARFETCH scale — a startup may require a different gear (more hours, more ambiguity, fewer support systems).

## The critical risk factor for Rui specifically

**16 months unemployed, benefits gone.** This changes the calculus:
- He cannot take a 3-year-zero-cash founding role. He needs income stability.
- A Series B startup at €150-200k cash is the minimum viable floor. Seed-stage at €100-120k + 2% equity is risky if he has no buffer.
- **Recommendation:** Target Series B with near-market cash (€170k+) and meaningful equity (0.5-1.5%). Treat the equity as a lottery ticket, not a retirement plan. The cash must cover living costs and rebuild savings.

## Decision framework

| Scenario | Cash | Equity | Risk | Verdict for Rui |
|----------|------|--------|------|-----------------|
| Corporate SVP (FARFETCH/Luxury brand) | €220-300k | 0% | Low | Safe but no upside; 16mo gap hurts |
| Series B AI-content startup | €170-210k | 0.5-1.5% | Medium | **Best balance for Rui** |
| Series A founding ops (AI-content) | €120-160k | 1.5-2.5% | High | Good if cash floor is acceptable |
| Series C+ growth startup | €200-260k | 0.1-0.3% | Low-Medium | Safe-ish, smaller upside |
| Seed founding COO | €100-130k | 2-5% | Very high | Too risky given 16mo unemployment |

## Bottom line
The startup path makes sense for Rui IF: (a) the cash floor is €170k+ to rebuild stability, (b) the equity is at least 0.5% at Series B or 1.5% at Series A, (c) the role is in AI-content or D2C where his FARFETCH playbook is directly applicable, and (d) the founders are credible and the round is well-funded (18+ months runway). He should NOT take a seed-stage role given his current financial position.
